ABSTRACT: Residential electricity demand forecasting plays a pivotal role in enabling energy conservation, preventing billing shocks, and supporting grid stability. This paper presents PowerTrack, an end-to-end empirical time-series forecasting and energy intelligence framework built on high-resolution smart-meter telemetry from the Council on Energy, Environment and Water (CEEW) dataset hosted on Harvard Dataverse, comprising 56,277 daily observations across 84 households in Mathura and Bareilly, Uttar Pradesh. We perform chronological stationarity testing (ADF and KPSS), STL decomposition, and lag autocorrelation analysis (ACF/PACF). We benchmark classical time-series models (Seasonal Naive, 7-Day Moving Average, Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing, ARIMA(1,1,1), and SARIMA(1,0,1)×(1,1,1)7) against advanced machine learning models (Feature-Engineered Ridge Autoregression, XGBoost Ensemble, and LSTM Neural Networks) using strict chronological Train-Validation-Test splitting (75%/15%/10%) to eliminate lookahead data leakage. Experimental results demonstrate that SARIMA(1,0,1)×(1,1,1)7 achieves the optimal performance on validation RMSE (1.805 kWh) and delivers a held-out test RMSE of 0.97 kWh (76.0% accuracy), while the advanced XGBoost model achieves comparable test RMSE (1.02 kWh). Furthermore, predictions are coupled with Uttar Pradesh Power Corporation Limited (UPPCL) domestic tariff slabs (Schedule LMV-1) to provide actionable budget limits and what-if simulation for individual households.

1. Introduction

The global transition toward decentralized smart grids has accelerated the rollout of advanced metering infrastructure (AMI). In India, the national Smart Meter National Programme (SMNP) and Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS) aim to replace 250 million legacy mechanical meters with smart meters. However, smart-meter telemetry is currently utilized almost exclusively in a retrospective manner by power distribution companies (DISCOMs) for automated billing and revenue collection. Individual residential consumers receive little to no actionable, predictive intelligence regarding their energy burn rates, leading to unexpected end-of-month "bill shocks."

Electricity consumption in developing regions is characterized by severe non-stationarity, erratic standby loads, and pronounced climate-driven surges (summer air-conditioning spikes vs. winter heating). A need exists for a consumer-centric, transparent analytics platform that ingests raw smart-meter telemetry, performs statistical validation, accurately forecasts short- and medium-term demand with confidence intervals, and translates physical kilowatt-hours into monetary costs under regional telescopic slab schedules.

Problem Statement (Real-World Consumer Perspective)

Electricity is an essential utility for everyday domestic living, yet ordinary households operate with virtually zero visibility into their daily energy spending. Consumers face four critical real-world challenges:

  • The "Bill Shock" Dilemma: Families only discover their electricity consumption when the paper bill arrives at the end of the billing cycle. By that time, the energy is consumed and the money is owed, making it impossible to adjust habits, curb waste, or prevent financial distress.
  • Hidden Tariff Slab Traps: In Indian states like Uttar Pradesh, electricity is billed in non-linear "telescopic tiers" where unit rates jump sharply (from ₹5.50 up to ₹7.00/kWh) as total consumption climbs. Most consumers do not realize that crossing a slab threshold by even a few units near month-end drastically escalates their total bill.
  • One-Way Smart Meters: Millions of digital smart meters have been deployed under national schemes, but the high-frequency telemetry is harvested exclusively by power distribution companies for billing and remote disconnections. Everyday residents receive no proactive alerts, no consumption projections, and no digital tools to understand their energy habits.
  • Lack of Actionable Conservation Guidance: Even when households actively wish to save money, they cannot determine which appliances (e.g., air conditioners, water heaters, refrigerators) drive their costs, nor do they know the practical financial savings of simple actions such as setting their AC thermostat 1°C higher.

Proposed Solution & Project Objectives

To resolve these consumer challenges, PowerTrack transforms raw smart-meter telemetry into an intelligent, consumer-friendly home energy assistant. The key objectives are to:

  • Provide Real-Time Cost Transparency: Convert confusing kilowatt-hours (kWh) into immediate daily costs in rupees (₹) so families understand their daily energy burn rate.
  • Deliver Early-Warning Bill Forecasting: Predict upcoming 7-day to 30-day electricity consumption and expected bill totals in advance, allowing households to budget without unexpected surprises.
  • Act as an Intelligent Budget Guardian: Alert consumers proactively before their daily consumption pace pushes them into expensive, higher-tier tariff slabs.
  • Empower Households with a What-If Simulator: Enable families to test practical conservation measures (e.g., thermostat adjustments, LED conversions) and instantly see the rupee savings on their monthly bill.

3. Dataset Description & Preprocessing Pipeline

The dataset originates from the Council on Energy, Environment and Water (CEEW) smart-meter study hosted on Harvard Dataverse (DOI: 10.7910/DVN/GOCHJH, Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Public Domain). It tracks 84 residential households across two districts in Uttar Pradesh: Bareilly (46 households) and Mathura (38 households), spanning May 2019 to October 2021 (over 2.5 years, comprising 56,277 daily records and 550,560 hourly interval records).

Preprocessing & Engineering Pipeline

  1. Datetime Normalization: Raw timestamps were parsed into standardized timezone-aware pandas DatetimeIndex series.
  2. Duplicate & Physical Filtering: Redundant transmissions were removed; non-physical negative energy readings were clipped (consumption_kwh ≥ 0).
  3. Time-Weighted Linear Imputation: Intermittent blackouts and sensor disconnects were handled by enforcing a regular daily frequency (asfreq('D')) and applying monotonic time-weighted linear interpolation bounded by forward/backward fills.
  4. Data Integrity Grading: Households were graded based on observation completeness: Grade A (≥90%), Grade B (75%–89%), and Grade C (<75%).
  5. Parquet Caching: Cleaned data was serialized into compressed Apache Parquet tables, reducing startup latency from 3.8s to <80ms.

4. Methodology & Model Architecture

To eliminate future-lookahead data leakage, data was partitioned chronologically: 75% Training (parameter fitting), 15% Validation (hyperparameter tuning and tournament selection), and 10% strictly Held-Out Test (final independent evaluation).

Models Formulated:

  • Seasonal Naive Baseline: ŷt+h = yt+h-7 (repeats the identical day of the preceding week).
  • 7-Day Moving Average: ŷt+h = (1/7) ∑ yt-i+1.
  • Holt-Winters Triple Exponential Smoothing: Additive trend and additive 7-day seasonality with optimized level (α), trend (β), and seasonal (γ) parameters.
  • Classical ARIMA(1, 1, 1): (1 - φ1B)(1 - B)yt = (1 + θ1B)εt.
  • Seasonal ARIMA — SARIMA(1, 0, 1)×(1, 1, 1, 7):
    (1 - Φ1B7)(1 - φ1B)(1 - B7)yt = (1 + Θ1B7)(1 + θ1B)εt
    Integrates non-seasonal AR(1), MA(1) and seasonal differencing (D=1, s=7), seasonal AR(1), and seasonal MA(1).
  • Ridge Autoregressive ML: Regularized linear model incorporating lags (t-1, t-2, t-7), 7-day rolling mean, and cyclical calendar transforms: sin(2π·DoW/7) and cos(2π·DoW/7).
  • XGBoost Regressor: Gradient-boosted decision trees (100 estimators, max_depth=4, learning_rate=0.05) trained on 14-day recursive lags.
  • LSTM Recurrent Neural Network: 2-layer sequence-to-sequence model (14-day input window, 32 units, Adam optimizer, 50 epochs).

Prediction Intervals (95% Confidence):

ŷt+h ± 1.96 · σresiduals · (1 + 0.04 √h)

The factor √h expands the confidence cone over the forecast horizon (7, 14, 30 days) to reflect compounding stochastic uncertainty.

Analytical Interpretation: Why Performance Differs

  • Why SARIMA Won: Residential electricity consumption exhibits a dominant weekly cycle (s=7). Seasonal differencing (D=1) effectively strips weekly seasonality and stabilizes non-stationary variance, enabling the model to capture daily fluctuations accurately with only 4 fitted parameters.
  • Why Classical ARIMA Failed: Standard ARIMA(1,1,1) only models correlations between adjacent days (t-1) and lacks seasonal awareness, treating weekly weekend shifts as random white noise (RMSE: 2.301 kWh vs. 1.805 kWh).
  • Advanced ML Capability (Ridge & XGBoost): Advanced models matched within 2.5% of SARIMA. Supplying explicit cyclical calendar features (&sin;/&cos; day-of-week) and rolling statistics enabled tree ensembles and regularized regression to successfully replicate seasonal behavior.
  • Why LSTM Underperformed on Individual Households: LSTM achieved an RMSE of 1.940 kWh. Deep neural networks require tens of thousands of observations; for individual households with 300 to 772 records, the large weight matrix of LSTM tends to slightly overfit sensor noise and blackout artifacts.

Stationarity & Diagnostic Test Scores

  • Raw Series (MH01): ADF Stat = -2.477, p-value = 0.1211 (Non-stationary; fail to reject H0).
  • After Differencing (d=1, s=7): ADF Stat = -10.470, p-value = 0.0000 (Strongly stationary; rejects H0 at 1% significance, critical value -3.438).
  • Post-Diff KPSS: Stat = 0.009, p-value = 0.1000 (Confirms stationary status).
  • STL Strength Metrics: Trend Strength Ftrend = 0.831; Seasonal Strength Fseason = 0.078.

6. Real-World Domain Application: Tariff Engine & Energy Intelligence

Unlike abstract models that predict kilowatt-hours without context, PowerTrack couples predictions directly with the official Uttar Pradesh Power Corporation Limited (UPPCL Schedule LMV-1 Domestic Urban) tariff schedule:

Total Bill (₹) = Energy Charges + Fixed Capacity Charges + State Electricity Duty (5%)
Consumption Tier (Slab) Slab Range Rate (₹ / kWh) Domestic Context
Slab 1 0 to 100 kWh ₹ 5.50 / kWh Subsidized lifeline consumption tier.
Slab 2 101 to 150 kWh ₹ 6.00 / kWh Basic domestic usage.
Slab 3 151 to 300 kWh ₹ 6.50 / kWh Standard multi-appliance household tier.
Slab 4 Above 300 kWh ₹ 7.00 / kWh High-consumption tier (steep cost escalation).
  • Fixed Capacity Charges: ₹ 110.00 per kW sanctioned load per month (Default 2.0 kW = ₹ 220.00/month).
  • State Electricity Duty: 5% government surcharge applied to total energy charges.
  • Budget Guardian: Evaluates burn rate (kWh/day) and projects month-end spending to warn consumers before jumping into Slab 4.
  • What-If Simulator: Models physical appliance savings (e.g., +1°C AC thermostat setpoint saves ~6% cooling power).
  • Statistical Anomaly Detection: Evaluates hourly readings using Z-score (|Z| > 2.5) and IQR bounds to detect power surges and standby leaks.

7. Conclusion, Limitations & Future Scope

PowerTrack successfully demonstrates an end-to-end smart-meter forecasting and energy intelligence framework on real Indian residential data. Chronological benchmarking confirmed SARIMA(1,0,1)×(1,1,1)7 as the winning model (0.97 kWh test RMSE, 76.0% accuracy), while feature-engineered XGBoost and Ridge ML proved highly effective. Integrating predictions with UPPCL telescopic tariffs empowers consumers to proactively manage their energy costs.

Limitations: Ambient temperature and weather covariates were omitted due to public dataset constraints. Disaggregation is estimated from hourly load shapes rather than individual appliance CT hardware clamps.

Future Work: Incorporate real-time India Meteorological Department (IMD) temperature telemetry via SARIMAX or Temporal Fusion Transformers (TFT), and integrate high-frequency Non-Intrusive Load Monitoring (NILM).

Key Defense Points for Viva (Cheat Sheet)

  • Why chronological split? Shuffling time series causes future data to leak into the past. Chronological splitting (75% Train, 15% Val, 10% Test) ensures zero data leakage.
  • Why RMSE as primary metric? RMSE squares errors before averaging, penalizing severe electricity demand spikes much more heavily than MAE.
  • Why Bounded MAPE? Household standby power drops near zero at night; standard MAPE divides by zero causing infinite errors. Minimum denominator of 0.5 kWh guarantees stability.
